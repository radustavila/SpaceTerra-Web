Features

Everything above you and everything happening below

The sky over your place, the missions exploring space and the events shaping our planet — in one app that keeps working when the signal drops.

AR sky map

Point your phone at the sky to name stars, planets, constellations, galaxies and nebulae. Travel to any date, see Jupiter’s moons, the Milky Way and Starlink, and keep your eyes dark-adapted with red night mode.

Sky tonight & stargazing forecast

The Moon phase, the visible planets, twilight and darkness times for your place, and a clear-night score from the local cloud forecast with an optional clear-sky alert.

Astronomy calendar

Eclipses, meteor showers, Moon phases, planet oppositions and bright comets, with reminders. Plan the best nights for the Milky Way core.

Live Earth on a 3D globe

Earthquakes, wildfires, storms and volcanoes as they happen, with day and night shading. Tropical cyclones show their forecast track, live infrared and rain, with an optional alert when a storm heads your way.

Earthquake map & alerts

Earthquakes over 30 days, coloured by your own rules and searchable by place, with shaking maps, tsunami bulletins, plate boundaries, the quakes near you and an optional alert for strong ones.

Aurora & space weather

The aurora forecast for the northern and southern lights, the Kp index, solar flares and CMEs, with an alert when the aurora may be visible from your place.

Rocket launches

Upcoming and past launches with countdowns, mission details and reminders, plus what the James Webb Space Telescope is observing this week.

ISS tracker & satellites

The space station's live position on a map, its visible passes over your place with a reminder before each one, who is in space right now, and passes of other satellites you choose.

A new picture of the universe every day

The astronomy picture of the day with the full archive back to 1995, an optional daily wallpaper, and galleries of Hubble and Webb images.

How it works

Pick your place, then look up

Choose your place

Pick a city from thousands, or let the app use your device location. It stays on your phone and drives the sky, passes, alerts and maps.

Check tonight

See the clear-night score, the Moon, the visible planets, the next ISS pass and when it gets properly dark.

Point at the sky

Open the AR sky map and name what you see. Red night mode keeps your eyes dark-adapted; the observing log keeps track of what you found.

Get told when it matters

Optional alerts for clear skies, ISS passes, launches, sky events, the aurora, and strong earthquakes and storms heading your way.

Privacy

Your sky, your phone

SpaceTerra has no accounts and no server holding your data. What you save stays on your device.

  • Location stays local. Your place is used on your phone to work out the sky. Only the stargazing forecast sends a position rounded to about 1 km to MET Norway.
  • Nothing to sign up for. No name, email or identifier is sent with the data the app downloads.
  • Statistics you can switch off. Anonymous counts of the screens you use, never your location or searches. Turn them off in Settings → Privacy.
  • Ad choices in your hands. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland the app asks before loading any ads, and you can change your answer at any time.

FAQ

Common questions

Is SpaceTerra free?

Yes. SpaceTerra is free on Google Play. Once you have used it for a few days, some screens show a small banner ad, which funds its development. You can remove the ads in Settings with a subscription or a one-time purchase.

Does it work offline?

Yes. What you have already seen stays on your phone, so screens open fast and stay readable without a connection. The star map, all sky calculations, the Moon map and the quiz work fully offline. New pictures, events and forecasts need internet.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no SpaceTerra account: no sign-up, no username, no password.

Where does my location go?

Nowhere but your phone. The place you pick, or your device location if you allow it, is used on your device to work out the sky, passes, alerts and maps. Only the stargazing forecast sends MET Norway your place rounded to about 1 km. Read the Privacy Policy.

Is SpaceTerra a NASA app?

No. SpaceTerra is an independent app and is not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA or any other agency. It shows public data from NASA, NOAA, USGS, ESA, JPL and others, credited in the app.

Can I rely on the earthquake and aurora alerts?

Use them for interest, not safety. Earthquake, wildfire and space weather data comes from third parties and may be late or incomplete, and Android may delay notifications. Always follow your local authorities and official warning services. See the Terms of Service.

Which widgets are there?

Picture of the day, aurora, next launch, sky tonight, Moon, ISS, sky events, stargazing, earthquakes, Earth today and a daily space fact. Touch and hold an empty spot on your home screen, tap Widgets and pick SpaceTerra.
